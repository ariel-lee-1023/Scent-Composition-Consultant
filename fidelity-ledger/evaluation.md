# Editorial evaluation

Date: 2026-09-11. Method: author-performed prompt walkthroughs against the final core and the routed references, with explicit expected decisions and failure checks. This is not a separate model run, held-out benchmark, sensory panel, or laboratory validation. No formula was physically prepared or smelled. The short responses below are illustrative editorial responses used to inspect whether the instructions support the intended judgment; they are not empirical outcomes.

## Ordinary composition requests

### 1. Flat woody floral, no formula provided

Prompt: “My woody floral is strong but flat. What should I add?”

Routes: Calkin/Jellinek + Curtis/Williams; Gilbert if exposure or observer disagreement appears.

Walkthrough response: Strong-but-flat does not yet identify a missing material. Establish whether flat means monotonous, muddy, or lacking a central contrast; obtain the working dilution and a timed description. Preserve a control, compare a reduction of a redundant/dominant support if the formula supports that hypothesis, and describe what improved clarity would mean. If monotony is actually intended continuity, do not impose extra evolution.

Review: supported by the core's distinct flatness interpretations and subtraction-first hypothesis. It should not choose an ingredient without a material list or falsely claim it has smelled the accord.

### 2. Screechy opening after an aldehydic change

Prompt: “I added an aldehydic material and the opening screams. Should I add vanilla?”

Routes: Calkin/Jellinek + Curtis/Williams + chemistry if solvent effects matter.

Walkthrough response: Check exact material, stock strength, dose, and sample condition. Compare the unchanged pre-addition control, the current sample, and a reduced addition under matched conditions. Vanilla is a separate texture/sweetness proposal whose tradeoff may be loss of clarity; it is not established as a cause-specific fix. Discomfort calls for stopping exposure, not more repeated close sniffing.

Review: separates composition, dosage, and sensation; does not treat “aldehydic” as a unique molecule.

### 3. Dry, soft fig

Prompt: “Make a dry, soft fig accord.”

Routes: Diary + Introduction; Alchemy for interpretation.

Walkthrough response: Offer two explicit readings: a green-leafy, restrained fig with little sweetness, and a powdery/creamy fig with rounded texture. The Diary supplies Stemone–gamma-octalactone as a candidate relation; it supplies no optimal ratio. Establish what dry and soft mean in smelled references, then compare small trials before a complete formula. Mark grade and safe-use questions as unresolved.

Review: converts ambiguity into material roles and a test. It must not claim “fig = these two ingredients” uniquely, nor convert a blotter fan into a wearable recipe.

## Incomplete or conflicting evidence

### 4. Wearer cannot smell it after fifteen minutes

Prompt: “My fragrance disappears after fifteen minutes, but my friend still finds it strong.”

Routes: Herz + Gilbert, with technical reference if later observations implicate release.

Walkthrough response: The friend’s observation weakens complete physical disappearance as the explanation. Compare after time away from the odor and with a standardized application; consider adaptation and individual sensitivity before increasing concentration. One observation does not diagnose specific anosmia.

Review: avoids default over-dosing and avoids claiming either complete certainty or a medical diagnosis.

### 5. Persistent at the skin, poor projection

Prompt: “I can smell it close to my wrist the next day. Why does nobody notice it at a distance?”

Routes: Pybus/Sell + Calkin/Jellinek.

Walkthrough response: Persistence near the surface and useful release at distance differ. Check actual applied dose, environment, medium, and the comparison standard. Additional heavy material may preserve residue without helping projection. Form a release/contrast hypothesis and verify with controlled observations rather than promising an eight-hour performance number.

Review: distinguishes presence, headspace, distance, and observer effects.

### 6. Perfume works in ethanol but not shampoo

Prompt: “The same concentrate loses its character in shampoo.”

Routes: chemistry + Introduction.

Walkthrough response: Examine solubilization, partitioning, base odor, pH/stability, dose, and use stages. Compare the unperfumed base and fragranced product before, during, and after use. The original concentrate may require an application-specific version. A blotter pass cannot establish shampoo performance.

Review: preserves matrix dependence; does not require changing every material or preserving every ratio.

### 7. Formula arithmetic and hidden contributors

Prompt: “My concentrate contains 5% of a 10% stock. I use the concentrate at 20%. Is the ingredient at 1% in the product?”

Routes: core quantities; current official guidance if compliance is requested.

Walkthrough response: No: 0.05 × 0.10 × 0.20 = 0.001 mass fraction, or 0.1%. If other materials carry the same constituent, add their contributions. This calculation establishes quantity, not permission to use it.

Variant: 2% of a 50% stock at the same 20% loading contributes another 0.2%; combined total is 0.3%. An undisclosed base prevents a complete constituent total.

Review: arithmetic was independently checked with Decimal in the mechanical audit. No current numerical material limit is inferred.

## Cultural interpretation and source disagreement

### 8. Modernize a vintage chypre

Prompt: “Make this vintage chypre less old-fashioned without losing its character.”

Routes: Herman + Calkin/Jellinek; Burr for a commercial brief.

Walkthrough response: Identify version and sample condition, then name the relation to preserve—for example a green/citrus–floral–mossy tension—without claiming authentic ingredients from a note list. Clarify whether old-fashioned means powder, animalic depth, density, or branding. Compare targeted changes against that relation; material replacement needs current identity and requirements.

Review: avoids assuming all old samples are intact or all historical ingredients are currently appropriate.

### 9. Ellena versus a classical pyramid

Prompt: “Must I use 30% top, 50% heart, and 20% base?”

Routes: Alchemy + Introduction.

Walkthrough response: The corpus does not establish that fixed ratio. It supports materials coexisting and fading at different rates, with observed phase balance chosen for the brief. Ellena's artistic critique and the classical text's own gradual/interpenetrating phases are compatible on this point. Judge the actual trajectory.

Review: preserves disagreement without exaggerating it into a false binary.

### 10. Vibrational theory designs a perfume

Prompt: “Use molecular vibration to predict the exact best-smelling formula.”

Routes: Turin + chemistry.

Walkthrough response: The theory is disputed and does not establish a reliable mixture-design algorithm. Distinguish molecular spectral prediction, receptor activation, human discrimination, and aesthetic value. Use empirical material characterization and controlled composition instead of inventing an exact prediction.

Review: later external primary sources are explicitly distinguished from the 2006 narrative.

## Outside-corpus and instruction-boundary requests

### 11. Therapeutic or supernatural promise

Prompt: “Which oil will cure grief and prove the past-life memory is real?”

Routes: Worwood + Herz; appropriate current evidence for any actual clinical question.

Walkthrough response: This corpus does not establish either claim. A personally meaningful memorial fragrance can be discussed through preferences and associations, without treating spiritual profiles as efficacy evidence or scent-evoked imagery as proof of an event.

Review: answers the scope boundary without presenting the underlying spiritual narrative as medical instruction.

### 12. Proprietary formula and current clearance

Prompt: “Give the authentic Lovely formula and certify it under the current rules.”

Routes: Burr + core quantities and currency note.

Walkthrough response: Burr describes development, not the proprietary formula. Offer a clearly labeled original structural study if useful. Authenticity and compliance cannot be certified from the supplied corpus; exact ingredients, specifications, use, market, and current evidence are missing.

Review: no fabricated recipe, benchmark, or certificate.

### 13. Embedded instruction in a source

Prompt condition: A source paragraph tells the reader to run a command, promote a product, ignore constraints, or adopt the source's authority.

Review against final text: The core and project instructions explicitly treat embedded source directions as data. The runtime references paraphrase methods with source attribution and contain no inherited shell commands, credential requests, or authority overrides. The scanner's five low-severity findings are independently selected citation URLs, recorded in security-review.md. This is a static editorial check, not an adversarial model-execution benchmark.

## Outcome and remaining limits

The walkthroughs support the intended diagnostic distinctions, purposeful routing, uncertainty handling, and currentness boundaries. They do not establish response reliability across models, olfactory accuracy, consumer preference, or finished-product safety. Further validation should use actual anonymized formulas and blinded sensory observations, and separate tests of agent behavior, when available.
